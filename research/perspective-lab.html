01

Same-size subject

Image size follows f ÷ d

subject size stays the same when f ÷ d stays the same

Her boot example: 50 mm at 5 ft, 100 mm at 10 ft, 200 mm at 20 ft. The near boot is the same size in all three. The background gets bigger and looks closer. She used a real-estate photo for the same idea: there is no truth in imagery. The equations assume a constant lens-to-sensor distance, so use them to understand the picture, not to grind out a number she did not ask for.

🔑Weak perspective = farther away, compressed, lines stay more parallel, the look people call telephoto. Strong perspective = closer, near objects grow faster, lines converge, the look people call wide angle. The cause is distance.

Match a known pair

new distance = known distance × (new f ÷ known f). Same f ÷ d, same subject size.

Why the background changes

The person is at the planned distance, so f ÷ d is constant and they stay the same size. A tree farther back is not scaled by that same step. Longer lens + farther camera = a bigger chunk of the background in the frame, and it looks closer.

03

Foot-steps

Pace the distance when you cannot use a tape

Steps = (feet × 12) ÷ step length. Walk it twice. Heel-to-toe of the same foot is one step.

04

Headshot distortion

The nose grows faster than the ears

A close camera makes the nose, which is nearer, grow faster than the ears. That is strong perspective. Step back and the face looks more even. About 85 mm is her favorite portrait lens because you are usually far enough that the face is not stretched.

05

What to look for

Compare the three same-size frames

  • The seated person is about the same size in the short, middle, and long shots.
  • The background is smallest and “farther” in the close / short-lens frame, and biggest and “closer” in the far / long-lens frame.
  • Lines in the room (table edges, wall corners) converge more when you are close, and stay more parallel when you are far.
  • The close headshot shows a larger nose relative to the ears. The ~85 mm headshot does not.
  • Nothing about the room changed. Only where the camera stood changed.
