01

Same subject size, different lens

Part I: your partner fills half the frame, a tree sits behind them

Change the lens. The camera backs up to keep the subject the same size.

Camera to subject: Tree height in frame: Background width you see:

🔑Image size ∝ focal length ÷ object distance. Double the lens and double the distance, and your partner stays the same size. Going from 50 mm to 100 mm doubles your distance to your partner (2.7 → 5.4 m), but the tree only goes from 17.7 to 20.4 m away. It isn't twice as far, so it grows.

03

Headshot distortion

Part II: same head size, three lenses

Pick a lens. The camera moves until the head fills the frame.

Camera to eyes: Nose tip magnified: Ears / face width:

  • The nose is about 10 cm closer to you than the eyes, and the ears about 8 cm farther. Up close, 10 cm is a big share of the distance, so the nose gets magnified much more than the ears.
  • From far away, those few centimeters barely matter, so the face looks flatter and wider, which is closer to how we see people in conversation.
  • Her pick: about 85 mm. "You would never shoot a portrait with a wide-angle lens."

What to look for in your photos

The ideas behind the lab questions. Answer from your own pictures.

Nose and chin (portraits)Look bigger and stick out more with the short lens
Eyes, forehead, ears (portraits)Look smaller, set back. Ears can disappear and the face looks narrower.
  • Shooting at f/4 instead of f/11–16 would give less depth of field: the background would blur, and blur most with the long lens. That would hide the size comparison the lab is about.
  • From the lecture notes: image size is directly proportional to focal length and inversely proportional to object distance.
  • Remember: the lens doesn't cause any of this. Your distance does. The lens just lets you keep the subject the same size from that distance.
