02

Photometer vs radiometer

Two ways to measure the same light

Photometer

Measures light the way the human eye sees it. It has a filter shaped like the eye's sensitivity curve, so green-yellow light (around 555 nm) counts the most, and UV and infrared count as zero.

Units: lumens (flux), candelas (intensity), lux (illuminance). Your camera's light meter works like this.

Radiometer

Measures all the energy, including invisible UV and infrared, no matter what the eye sees.

Units: watts. A tungsten bulb scores high on a radiometer but low on a photometer, because most of its energy is invisible heat.

💡
Photo = light as people see it. The eye is most sensitive to green (555 nm) in daylight and shifts toward blue-green (507 nm) in the dark. That's why blue and green objects look relatively brighter at dusk (the Purkinje effect). Incandescent light is associated with heat because most of a tungsten bulb's energy is infrared.
