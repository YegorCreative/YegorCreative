Normal lens & crop factor

"Normal" depends on the sensor size

  • A normal lens reproduces a field of view that looks natural to a human: about 47°, measured on the diagonal.
  • Normal focal length ≈ the diagonal of the film or sensor: about 50 mm for 35 mm/full frame and about 150 mm for 4×5. For digital, it depends on the size of the chip.
crop factor = 43.3 mm ÷ sensor diagonal
diagonal = √(width² + height²)

    Full-frame lens vs crop lens

    Will the image circle cover the sensor?

    • A full-frame lens works on a crop body (same mount or with an adapter), and it acts longer: a 50 mm on a 2× body looks like 100 mm.
    • A crop lens on a full-frame body ❌: its image circle is too small, so the corners go dark. Upgrade to full frame later and you have to rebuy your lenses.
    • Her advice: "If you buy cheap, you buy twice." Borrow good gear from the cage.

    Focal lengthLonger than normal (e.g. 150 mm on FF; normal on 4×5!)Shorter than normal (<50 mm FF, <150 mm 4×5)
    MagnificationIncreasedDecreased

    🛡️Put a good UV or clear protective filter on expensive lenses. A cheap one causes ghosting.

    Same spot, different focal lengths

    The camera never moves: only the framing changes. At 18 mm you see the whole shore. At 200 mm the far ships fill the frame. Her slide used 18, 24, 35, 55, 70, 85, 100, 135 and 200 mm.

    04

    Telephoto vs reverse telephoto (retrofocus)

    "A very good test question"

    ★ Her test pick · “A very good test question”
    Telephoto vs reverse telephoto (retrofocus): how are the elements arranged?
    Telephoto: positive element in front, negative behind → rear nodal point falls in front of the lens.
    Retrofocus: negative in front, positive behind → rear nodal point falls behind the lens, so a wide angle clears the DSLR mirror.

    Normal telephoto

    Positive element in front, negative behind. The back nodal point falls in front of the lens, so a long focal length fits in a shorter barrel. The lens sits close to the sensor and mirror.

    Reverse telephoto = retrofocus

    Elements swapped: negative in front, positive behind. The back nodal point moves behind the lens group, so the lens sits farther from the mirror and doesn't hit it. Her slide calls it the "Reverse Telephoto Wide Angle Lens".

    Beyond the lectureRetrofocus is how wide-angle lenses for DSLRs are built. A plain 20 mm lens would have to sit 20 mm from the sensor, inside the mirror's swing. Retrofocus gives a short focal length with a long back-focus distance. Telephotos do the opposite: a long focal length in a short lens.
    05

    Framing vs perspective

    She "can't impose upon that enough"

    ★ Her test pick · She “can't impose upon that enough”
    What does changing the focal length change, and what does moving the camera change?
    Focal length → framing.
    Camera position → perspective.
    Change the LENS → change FRAMING
    Move the CAMERA → change PERSPECTIVE
    • Perspective = the relative size of objects in a scene, and it's controlled by camera position (distance).
    • Move closer and foreground objects become relatively larger. Everything at the same distance scales together.
    • Why people think lenses change perspective: with a telephoto you usually stand far away, and with a wide angle close. The distance is what changes the look.
    06

    Prime vs zoom

    One focal length or many

    Prime

    Fixed focal length, often a large maximum aperture ("fast"), optimized for that one focal length.

    Zoom (variable focal length)

    Moves lens groups inside to change the angle of view. Flexible framing, no lens swaps, but a more complex optical design. It may be less well corrected for aberrations, slower (smaller max aperture), and less sharp than a prime.

    Her slide: image quality, maximum aperture, size and cost depend on the specific lens design.

    07

    Macro lenses & reproduction ratio

    True macro = 1:1 or more

    • A macro lens focuses at very short distances for high magnification, and it's built to minimize distortion and correct flatness of field up close.
    • 1:1 (life size): a 15 mm ladybug is 15 mm on the sensor. Only lenses that reach 1:1 or more are true macro lenses.
    • 1:2: half life size. 2:1: twice life size.
    • The macro scale on the lens barrel shows 1:1, 1:2 and so on.
    08

    Supplemental lenses & diopters

    A cheap way to get closer

    D = 1 / f (f in METERS) · f = 1 / D
    diopters add: Dc = D + Ds

    Diopter calculator

    • A supplemental (close-up) lens screws onto the front of a lens. Its power is given in diopters, which are defined in meters.
    • Examples from her slide: 2 diopters → f = ½ m = 500 mm. 4 diopters → ¼ m = 250 mm.
    • A cheap, light option (great for travel), but its distortion correction is limited, and quality depends on the glass.
    • The number on the ring (e.g. 49 mm) is the thread diameter, not the focal length.
    • Lab: with one added you must move the camera closer to focus (her floor tiles are 1 ft each!).
    Beyond the lectureHer slide says a supplemental lens is a "cheap way of increasing focal length". Use her words on the exam. Strictly, a + diopter adds power, so the combined focal length gets shorter. That's what lets you focus closer, and the result is more magnification.
    09

    Specialty lenses

    Tilt-shift, mirror and soft focus

    Tilt-shift (perspective control)

    Shifts up and down or side to side to prevent converging lines (tall buildings leaning in). It's like a small piece of a 4×5 camera's movements, great for architecture and real estate. It can also fake "miniature" scenes in camera.

    Catadioptric (mirror, folded optics)

    Curved mirrors plus glass fold the light path, so you get a long focal length in a short, light lens (like telescopes). Drawbacks: usually one fixed f-stop (no depth-of-field control), the f-number ignores the light lost to the center mirror, so metering can be off, and you may need ND filters.

    Soft focus (portrait)

    Made by under-correcting spherical aberration. The softness is only around the edges; the center stays sharp. The effect shrinks as you stop down. It's not the same as defocusing (that's just blur).

    👁️Portraits: the eyes must be sharp. The rest can fall off.

    Tilt-shift: fix converging lines

    Catadioptric (mirror) lens: folded light path

    The dark secondary mirror blocks the center. That's why there's only one fixed f-stop, why the f-number doesn't account for the lost light, and why out-of-focus highlights look like little donuts.
