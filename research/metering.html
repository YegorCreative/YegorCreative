01

The "right" exposure & 18% gray

What your light meter is aiming for

Exam answerAn average scene (a light meter) is calibrated to 18% gray.
black18% gray (middle gray)white

A reflected light meter doesn't know what it's looking at. It assumes every scene averages out to middle gray and picks settings that make whatever it sees come out 18% gray. When the scene really is average, that gives a proper exposure, shown as EV 0 on the meter.

💡
The meter wants everything gray. That's all you need to remember to answer the white card, gray card and black card questions.

The meter always does the opposite: White → Under, Black → Over, Gray → Good. On the exam, write "properly exposed" or "correctly exposed", not "balanced".

04

Camera metering modes

Which part of the frame the camera's reflected meter reads

Beyond your review sheets: good to know, but it wasn't in the practice exam
Evaluative / Matrix

Reads the whole frame in many zones and averages them smartly.

Everyday shooting
Center-weighted

Reads the whole frame but gives the center the most importance.

Portraits, centered subjects
Partial

Reads a medium-sized area in the center (on some cameras).

Backlit subjects
Spot

Reads only a tiny spot (about 1–5% of the frame).

Metering off a gray card, stage lighting
💡
Every mode is still a reflected meter aiming for 18% gray. Spot mode on a gray card is the most reliable way to get a proper exposure with your camera's meter.
