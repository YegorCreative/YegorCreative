Color Checker

A color checker is a card of 24 carefully measured color patches. Photographers shoot it under their lighting to check color accuracy and set white balance. Explore the chart, see how different lights change it, and correct your own photo.

Row 1 = natural colors (skin, sky, foliage). Row 2 = hard-to-reproduce colors. Row 3 = primaries and secondaries. Row 4 = neutral grays from white to black, used for white balance. Patch 22 (Neutral 5) is close to 18% gray, the same middle gray your light meter aims for.

Colors are standard published sRGB reference values. Every screen shows them slightly differently, so for real color work, use a physical card.

Why does tapping gray fix the photo?

The middle-gray patch is perfectly neutral (no color) and is 18% gray, the same brightness a light meter aims for. If it looks orange, blue or green in your photo, the light added a color cast. The tool measures that patch and adjusts the red, green and blue channels until it's neutral again. That's white balance. With "Also fix brightness" on, it also brightens or darkens the whole photo until the patch is exactly middle gray. That's exposure.

Important: only tap something that is really gray or white in real life, like a gray card. If you tap sand, skin or a tan rock, the tool thinks that color is a mistake and pushes the whole photo the opposite way (usually too blue). For brightness, use the middle gray patch, not white. No gray card in your photo? Use Auto balance, which averages the whole scene instead, and lower the strength if it looks too strong.
