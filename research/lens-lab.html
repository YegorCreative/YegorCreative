What is a nodal point?

The lens's "pivot point" for light

A lens has two nodal points on its optical axis: a front (object) one and a rear (image) one. A ray aimed at the front nodal point comes out as if from the rear nodal point, parallel to its original direction. In a thin simple lens both sit together at the center. In a complex lens (several separated elements) they're in different places.

Why the image doesn't move

Light from a far-away bulb arrives parallel. If you rotate the lens around its rear nodal point, the focused spot stays put on the screen. Rotate around any other point and the lens slides sideways, so the spot moves.

Why we care

Focal length = distance from the rear nodal point to the sharp image when the object is at infinity. Find the nodal point and you can measure f. And for panoramas, rotating on the rear nodal point (on a nodal slide) gives less distortion than rotating on the tripod screw.

🎯Wiggle test: image still = you're on the nodal point. Image moves = slide the lens and try again. If it moves more, you went the wrong way.

Combined lenses (Part I)

Two lenses touching act like one stronger lens

1 / fcombined = 1 / f₁ + 1 / f₂

Calculator

    • Flipping a plano-convex lens (curve to the light vs curve to the screen) gives the same focal length, but its nodal point sits in a different spot, so it ends up in a different place relative to the pivot. Mark the lens position on your lab drawing.
    • Putting two plano-convex lenses flat to flat makes a double convex lens with about half the focal length (twice the power).
    Why measured ≠ calculated (and it's not "human error")The formula assumes infinitely thin lenses touching at one point. Real lenses have thickness, so their nodal (principal) planes are separated. With our practice lenses (8 mm thick each) the true combined focal length is about 52.8 mm, not 50. Other real causes: the bulb isn't truly at infinity, and the slide wobbles.

    Nodal points of a complex lens (Part II)

    Front and rear are NOT in the same place

    1. Front of lens facing the bulb → find the stationary image → that's the image (rear) nodal point over the pivot. Measure front edge of barrel → pivot.
    2. Reverse it (front facing the screen) → light now goes through backwards → you find the object (front) nodal point. Measure front edge → pivot again.
    3. The two distances differ, because the lens has separated element groups, so its two nodal points sit in different places. If you got the same number, you weren't critical enough with the wiggle, so do it again.

    The lab lens is an old Eastman Kodak portrait lens, about 12 inches (~305 mm), f/4, with a 180 mm barrel, so your measurements can't be more than 180 mm. The numbers in the drawing above are made up for practice.

      Handheld shutter speed rule (Part IV)

      Slowest speed you can hold steady

      slowest handheld shutter ≈ 1 / (focal length × crop factor)

      Rule calculator (practice numbers)

        Whole-stop shutter speeds: 1/1000 · 1/500 · 1/250 · 1/125 · 1/60 · 1/30 · 1/15 · 1/8 · 1/4 · 1/2 · 1 s. The lab's own example: full frame + 50 mm → 1/50 → closest whole stop 1/60 → start one stop faster at 1/125, then go slower one stop at a time until it's blurry.

        Shake simulator

        • Longer lens → harder to hold steady, so you need faster speeds. A shorter, lighter lens lets you go slower.
        • Image stabilization (in the lens, the body, or both) buys several stops.
        • On a tripod, turn IS off. Mechanical IS can "correct" movement that isn't there and cause vibration.
        07

        How to hold the camera steady

        From the lab sheet and Dan's demo

        🤲 Make a shelf

        Both hands on. Rest the body in your left palm and cradle the lens, using thumb and finger to zoom and focus.

        👁 Eye to the viewfinder

        Rest the eyepiece against your face. Don't shoot on live view at arm's length, because pressing the shutter pushes the camera down.

        🌬 Exhale, then shoot

        Breathe out, press, then breathe again. Never inhale and hold.

        💪 Elbows in

        Tuck your arms against your body. Don't "fly like a bird".

        🦶 Bipod stance

        One foot in front of the other. Feet together, you start to sway.

        🧎 Go low

        For a low angle, put one knee on the ground. Wrap the strap around your wrist.
