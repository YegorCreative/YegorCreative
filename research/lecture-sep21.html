01

How a digital camera works

A film camera with a sensor where the film was

  • Same lens, shutter and aperture as a film camera. The sensor replaced the film and translates the optical image into an electrical signal.
  • The ⦶ mark on top of your camera (a circle with a line through it) shows where the sensor (film plane) sits. It's handy for measuring image distance.
  • A sensor is a 2-D array of photodiodes (photosites). Each one collects photons and builds up a charge.

Photosite

On the sensor. It catches light.

Pixel

In the file. It's the number the photosite becomes after processing.

🎨Photosites are colorblind. A photon is a photon. They only "see" brightness, which is why we need a color filter array.
02

Analog → digital (A/D conversion)

Smooth voltage becomes steps

  • More light → more voltage. Voltage is analog: unlimited levels (1.375647 V is fine).
  • The analog-to-digital (A/D) converter sorts each voltage into a digital count. The number of counts is limited by the bit depth.
  • Every voltage in a range gets the same count. That's where stair-steps on smooth edges come from (you don't see them with enough pixels).

Bit depth: how many tones?

Each extra bit doubles the tones

BitsTonesNotes
1 (2¹)2black or white (bitonal: 0 = black, 1 = white)
2 (2²)4
3 (2³)8
4 (2⁴)16OK on busy (high-frequency) subjects
5 (2⁵)32looks fine to most people on a photo
8 (2⁸)256per channel; room for processing
16 (2¹⁶)65,536
24 (2²⁴)16.7 millioncolor: 8 bits × R, G, B

Low-frequency areas show problems

Smooth, flat areas (a plain wall, sky) change slowly, so too few bits shows as banding.

High-frequency areas hide problems

Busy detail (hair, lace, grass, fur) changes a lot from pixel to pixel, so a low bit depth is hard to notice.

  • Dithering mixes black and white dots to fake gray tones (newspaper style).
  • Why 8 bits if 5 looks OK? Editing a low-bit image shows artifacts right away. Fewer bits only make sense for small web or phone images where bandwidth matters.
Spotted on the slidesOne slide says "8 bits – 265 levels per channel". That's a typo: 2⁸ = 256. Write 256 on the exam.

A bitonal image is just 0s and 1s

1 bit per pixel: 0 = black, 1 = white. Fine for black-and-white text, tiny files.

Gray levels per number of bits

Each extra bit doubles the levels, so the bars barely show until about 12 bits, then they explode: 4,096 → 65,536.

Where you notice too few bits

Low frequency (smooth wall): bands show

High frequency (grass, fur): steps hide

04

Histograms: check all four

Luminance + red, green and blue

Cameras show a luminance (composite) histogram, and often the separate R, G and B histograms. The in-camera histogram is based on the JPEG, even when you shoot raw.

📊Look at all of them. The combined brightness can look fine while one color channel is clipped.

Expose to the right (her slide)

  • Applies specifically to digital raw capture
  • Base proper exposure on the highlights
  • Get the histogram snug against the right (255) without going over
  • It may look bright, but that's solved in editing
  • A raw sensor has a linear response (not true for JPEG): less visible noise in the highlights, more in the shadows

Quantum efficiency: how much light becomes signal

That's why astronomers adopted CCDs so fast.

Fun facts: 1970 = the first digital image (the photoelectric effect). 1974: Fairchild's first 2-D device was only 100 × 100 pixels. Sensors are made on silicon wafers in clean rooms; one piece of lint ruins one.

Readout race: CCD conveyor belt vs CMOS

Each square is one photosite's charge. Press a button.

Why CMOS needs microlenses

CMOS puts an A/D converter at each photosite, and that circuitry takes up space. A tiny lens on top funnels the light into the sensitive part.

06

The Bayer pattern: where it came from

Kodak, 1976

  • Invented by Bryce Bayer of Kodak (patent 1976). Say "BUY-er", not "bear".
  • A color filter array (CFA) of red, green and blue filters over the photosites, with twice as many green to match the eye's sensitivity.
  • Won an Emmy in 2001 (National Academy of Television Arts and Sciences).
  • Each filter throws away about 2/3 of the light, so 2/3 of every color image is made-up data → demosaicing (see the Sep 28 tab).
Kodak storyKodak led early digital imaging but held onto film (about 800% markup). Its sensor division even made Leica's sensors.
