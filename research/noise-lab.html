SNR basics

Signal compared with noise, in decibels

SNR = 20 × log₁₀( Mean ÷ Std Dev )
Mean = signal

The average pixel value of the patch, which is its real brightness. The gray patch sits around the midtones and the black patch in the shadows.

Std Dev = noise

How much the pixels jump around the average. A perfectly smooth patch would have Std Dev 0. More grain means a bigger Std Dev.

💡
High SNR = clean, low SNR = noisy. Every time Mean ÷ Std Dev gets 10× bigger, SNR goes up by 20 dB: a ratio of 10 = 20 dB, 100 = 40 dB, 1000 = 60 dB.

05

ISO vs pushing exposure in post

Lab Part IV: a proper exposure at high ISO vs underexposing at low ISO and brightening later

ISO 1600, metered exposure
Shadow SNR
Nothing changed in post.
ISO 200, same f/ & shutter → 3 stops under, +3 in Lightroom
Shadow SNR
1600 → 800 → 400 → 200 = 3 stops underexposed.
💡
Both photos got the same amount of light, so the basic noise from the light itself is the same. But brightening in post also multiplies the camera's own electronic (read) noise, so the shadows usually look worse in the pushed image. On many cameras, using the higher ISO at the metered exposure gives cleaner shadows. Some newer sensors are nearly "ISO-invariant", so the difference is small.

💡
Bigger pixels catch more light (like a wider bucket in the rain), so the signal is stronger compared with the noise and the SNR is higher. Switch the Sensor buttons in section 02 to see the same ISO get noisier on smaller sensors. To find your own camera's pixel size, divide the sensor width (mm) by the image width in pixels, then × 1000 to get µm.
